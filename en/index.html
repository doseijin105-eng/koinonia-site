Sharing a meal, worship, God’s Word and prayer.

ABOUT

About Tomonari Arai

Born in Nagoya in 1983, Tomonari grew up in a Christian home and was baptized at Christmas when he was 20. After working as a computer instructor, he committed his life to ministry in 2012. Today he is associate pastor and next-generation ministry worker at Asahi Bible Church, and a staff partner of KGK (the Japanese member of IFES), walking with university students.

What matters most to him is to be “someone who understands and stays close,” taking time with each person. Through being left behind by friends as a student and losing someone he loved, he learned how painful it is to have no place to belong. So the heart of his ministry is this: not leaving lonely people behind, creating a safe place for them, and staying there with them.

His other role is to connect churches and people. Moving between churches inside and outside his denomination (JECA), KGK, interdenominational youth gatherings and rural churches, he links young people and churches across denominations and regions.

Where he servesMainly Nagoya and the four Tokai prefectures (Aichi, Gifu, Mie and Shizuoka), and anywhere in Japan when there is a need.

TESTIMONY

Testimony

How I came to faith

I grew up in a Christian home, and church felt like my home. Yet from childhood I had low self-esteem and felt a kind of loneliness. At camps I heard many testimonies, but I kept wondering, “Does God really love me?”

I failed every university I applied to and spent a year as a rōnin student. All my close friends had moved on, and I felt left behind and lost. That November, through my own carelessness, my rabbit Cook, whom I had kept for eleven years, could no longer walk. I prayed desperately, but in January he died in my arms. I was angry with God: “Why would you do this?”

At a KGK summer camp, during a time of quiet reading alone, I read the book of Jonah. God said to the angry Jonah that He could not help but pity the great city of Nineveh, “more than 120,000 people … and also much cattle.” I realized that I was the one who deserved to perish because of my sin, yet God loved me. More than I wanted Cook to live, God wanted me to live, and He gave His Son on the cross to save me. I received Jesus Christ as my Savior, and that Christmas I was baptized. The sorrow over Cook has not fully healed, but I want to live and serve as one who is kept alive by God’s Word.

How I was called

In graduate school I decided to stop living for myself and follow Jesus. Seeing friends drift away from church after graduation, I opened my rented home for meals and Bible study, praying, “Lord, if this is not your will, take it away.”

I then worked for three years and learned that the Lord never abandons those who trust Him. In January 2012, anxious about the future, I was given Jesus’ words, “Do not be afraid; from now on you will be catching people,” and the disciples left everything and followed Him. Through Psalm 119:9 and 2 Timothy 3–4, I resolved to give myself to proclaiming God’s Word so that people in the church, especially the young, would be equipped for every good work. In February 2012 I committed my life to ministry.

Grace in weakness

In April 2020, under the pressures of the pandemic, I fell into depression and insomnia, and my body and mind broke down. From that September I took five months of leave. Church members and pastor friends stayed beside me, and little by little I recovered.

It took almost two years of prayer to reconfirm my calling. By God’s grace I returned to pastoral ministry. Because I have walked through weakness, I now want to stay close to young people who are struggling and to churches that are tired.

My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.2 Corinthians 12:9

PET LOSS CARE

Walking with those who lose a pet

Saying goodbye to Cook, the rabbit I lived with for eleven years, was one of the turning points of my faith. Today more and more people who have lost a pet ask me, “How can we say goodbye in a Christian way?” I have begun a ministry of walking with them in prayer as they grieve a companion who was part of the family.

A Christian farewell prayer

At home or at church: Scripture, prayer, and if desired a hymn and time to share memories. Anyone may ask, with or without faith.

A joint memorial for beloved pets

A gathering of people who have lost an animal companion, to thank God who gave that life and to pray for comfort.

Studying a theology of animals

Animals are lives that God made and cares for. I study Scripture, church history and current discussion so that I can comfort people with honest words.

What we hold to

  • We pray to God the Creator who gave the life. We do not worship the animal, and this is not a Buddhist memorial rite (kuyō).
  • We do not claim more than the Bible clearly says about a pet’s life after death or reunion; we entrust it to God’s goodness and mercy.
  • Faith and giving are never conditions for receiving comfort. We do not decide how deep or how long someone’s grief should be.
  • We keep clear what is personal ministry and what the church supports, so the work can continue without strain.

Pet funerals that include cremation are being prepared, working with licensed specialists and checking the necessary procedures. Details and costs will be announced once they are settled.

Not even one sparrow is forgotten by God.From Luke 12:6

WHY KOINONIA

Why a place for fellowship?

Christians make up only about 1% of Japan’s population. Churches have few young people, and those who come are busy with study and work, with little time to talk deeply. Yet many young people, Christian or not, long for real, personal friendship. This spiritual hunger is the biggest need we see.

Few young people in church

Many churches have no believers of the same age, and young Christians feel alone.

Busy lives, few chances for fellowship

Study, work and church duties leave no time to talk honestly, even after worship.

A hunger for depth

Many long for friends who will share their struggles and pray with them.

No safe opportunity or place

Inside and outside church, young people have almost nowhere to share their weaknesses and real feelings safely.

So: a safe, unhurried, deep place

  • Safe — beyond church and denominational lines, a place where you can be weak and still be welcomed
  • Unhurried — from Friday evening to Saturday, sharing meals and talk without watching the clock
  • Deep — sharing God’s Word and prayer, walking alongside each person’s life

Koinonia will keep providing this opportunity and place regularly, a home young people can always come back to.

Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.Matthew 11:28

KOINONIA

Koinonia

Koinonia is a gathering where young people from many churches and denominations share a meal, worship, and share God’s Word and prayer. We want it to be a place where young people who feel alone in their faith can be weak and still be welcomed.

  • RetreatsFriday evening to Saturday: worship, a message and sharing, prayer, dinner, the public bath and late-night ramen
  • New Year retreatsGreeting the new year together in prayer
  • DinnersEvening meals with worship, a short message and sharing
  • Gatherings elsewhereTokyo, Gifu, Nagano and Ise-Shima

The July 2025 retreat had 43 applicants, the most ever. In every survey, 60–80% say a gathering like this is “very much needed.”

NEXT STAGE

The next stage

Seventeen young people have now joined ten or more gatherings, and one in three who sign up offers to help. We thank God for growing this fellowship.

To be honest, there are challenges. In 2026 only zero to three newcomers came to each gathering, about 40% come only once, and most of the planning, driving and cooking rests on Tomonari alone. He tends to carry too much with his own time and body. He took leave in 2020 and was given a year of rest in 2022. Today he keeps a weekly day off and is not taking new requests until March 2027.

Raise up leaders

Hand planning to the core members, with Tomonari as mentor and prayer partner.

Open new doors

Dinners and gatherings that newcomers and friends can easily join.

Keep one focus

Fellowship with and mentoring of young adults and students; serving rural churches from there.

A circle of prayer and counsel

We hope the support group will be not only financial support, but a circle of friends who pray and give counsel, so that this work can continue for the long run.
