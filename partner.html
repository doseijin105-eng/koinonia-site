Why Japan, and why young adults?

Christians make up only about 1% of Japan's population. Many congregations are small and aging, and many have no pastor. A young believer may be the only person of their generation in their church.

Those who do come are busy with study, long working hours and church duties. After Sunday worship there is little time to talk honestly. Some quietly drift away after graduation. Yet many young people, Christian or not, long for real, unhurried friendship where they can be weak and still be welcomed.

Few peers

Many churches have no believers of the same age, so young Christians feel alone.

Busy lives

Study, work and church duties leave little time for deep conversation.

Few safe places

There are few opportunities, inside or outside church, to share struggles and pray together.

What Koinonia does

Koinonia began in October 2022 in Nagoya. Retreats run from Friday evening to Saturday: worship, a message and small-group sharing, prayer, dinner, a trip to the public bath and late-night ramen. There are also New Year retreats, evening dinners, and gatherings in Tokyo, Gifu, Nagano and Ise-Shima.

About Tomonari

Born in Nagoya in 1983 and raised in a Christian home, Tomonari came to assurance of faith through KGK (the Japanese member movement of IFES) and was baptized at Christmas 2003. After graduate school and three years working as a computer instructor, he committed his life to ministry in 2012. He trained at the Japan Bible Seminary in Tokyo and served a church in the Kanto region for four years.

Today he is associate pastor and next-generation ministry worker at Asahi Bible Church in Nagoya (Japan Evangelical Church Association). One or two days a week he walks with university students as a KGK staff partner. He preaches at small churches across the Tokai region (Aichi, Gifu, Mie and Shizuoka) and beyond, wherever there is a need.

He is honest about his limits: in the past he carried too much and needed a season of rest. Today he keeps a weekly day off and is choosing one clear focus.

Questions

Who receives and manages the gifts?

Gifts support Tomonari's living and ministry costs. They are overseen by the Support Group for Rev. Tomonari Arai, based at Asahi Bible Church in Nagoya, which also sends regular newsletters to supporters.

Is my gift tax-deductible?

Gifts are not tax-deductible outside Japan at this time.

Can I give in my own currency?

Yes. Gifts are charged in yen, and your card company converts them. Visa, Mastercard, American Express, JCB, Diners Club, Discover and UnionPay are accepted.

Can I stop or change a monthly gift?

Yes, any time. Just write to us.

Can our church or group partner with Koinonia?

We would love that. You can pray using the card above, invite Tomonari to share by video call, or connect Japanese students and returnees in your area with Koinonia when they move to the Nagoya area.

How can I receive news?

Write to us and we will send our English newsletter.
